# Marketing campaign review survey

Introduction: We are researching how marketing teams review campaigns before launch. This voluntary survey takes about 3 minutes. Please do not include customer information, confidential campaign copy or personal data. Responses will inform product research; they are not testimonials. Before publishing, add the research owner's contact, retention period and privacy information. No responses have been collected by this repository.

1. What best describes your role? (Single choice: agency marketer; in-house marketer; legal/compliance; founder; other; prefer not to say.)
2. How many campaigns do you review per month? (0; 1–5; 6–20; 21–50; 51+.)
3. Which checks do you perform? (Checkboxes: legal claims; factual consistency; broken links/conversion; accessibility; brand trust; none; other.)
4. How do you currently review campaigns? (Checkboxes: manual checklist; legal reviewer; browser tools; AI tool; no formal process; other.)
5. How long does a typical review take? (<15 minutes; 15–60 minutes; 1–4 hours; >4 hours; unsure.)
6. In the last three months, how often did a campaign need changes after launch due to an overlooked issue? (Never; once; 2–5 times; more than 5; unsure; prefer not to say.)
7. Which part of review is most difficult, and why? (Optional short text.)
8. How useful would a pre-launch report linking possible risks to evidence and proposed fixes be? (1 not useful to 5 very useful; unsure.)
9. What would prevent adoption? (Checkboxes: false alerts; missed issues; privacy; integrations; price; unclear legal reliability; nothing; other.)
10. What would you need to verify before trying it? (Optional short text.)
11. What monthly budget, if any, would you consider for such a tool? (EUR 0; 1–49; 50–199; 200–499; 500+; cannot assess.)

Google Forms setup: Do not collect emails or require sign-in by default. Keep free text optional. Disable public response summaries. Pilot with 2–3 reviewers to identify confusing questions. Report sample size, recruitment method and missing answers; do not imply representativeness. Any follow-up contact should use a separate opt-in form with its own privacy notice.
